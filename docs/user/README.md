## How Jiary sees your day

Your day is a list of **sessions**. A session is a stretch of time spent on
one thing: when it started and ended, what kind of work it was, and what
it was about. Only one session runs at a time, and starting a new one ends
the running one.

A session has:

- an **activity** (required): the kind of work, such as Programming,
  Reading or Meeting;
- a **project** and a **task** (both optional): what the work was for;
- **notes**, added while it runs;
- an **interruption** count, for small distractions;
- a **focus** rating from 1 to 3, given right after it ends.

Activities, projects and tasks are free text. Type a new name and it exists.
Jiary suggests the names you've used before, but it doesn't manage them:
nothing has a status, a deadline or an owner.

Three activities are **detours**, things you step into from your work and
come back from: **Disruption**, **Break** and **Meeting**. Each has its own
key, and `r` takes you back to the work you left.

## What Jiary won't do

Jiary records what happened. It doesn't manage what should happen. The diary
is the source of truth, and timers are just a way to record it accurately.
Sessions are things that happened, not workflow items. So there are no to-do
lists, priorities, reminders, goals or streaks. It doesn't track habits
either. It has no accounts and no sync, and it never uses the network. Your
diary is one file on your computer.
