Jiary 1.1.0 adds a way back to any past day, a Pomodoro that never
interrupts, and sleep and mood to set your days' work against.

## New

- **`/rewind`: every past day, searchable.** A list of every day you
  tracked, newest first, under the year's focus grid. Type to search:
  plain words look in activities, projects, tasks and notes; `a:` `p:` `t:`
  `n:` narrow a word to one of them; quotes make a phrase; and dates
  (`2026-10-23`, `2026-10`, `2026`, `d:oct`) narrow the days. What matched
  is underlined, and the grid lights only the matching days. `Enter` opens a
  day as the main screen showed it, and `←` `→` step through the list.
- **A soft Pomodoro.** Press `p`: a thin bar under the timer fills toward
  your target, then the timer turns green and the cat has a coffee. It
  never interrupts: no sound, no notification, and the session keeps
  running.
- **`/settings`** for the Pomodoro's mode and target (5 minutes to 2 hours)
  and for sleep and mood.
- **Sleep and mood.** The day's first `s` asks how you slept and how you
  feel, one key each (`Esc` skips for the day). The week, month and year
  views then show your focus after good and poor sleep, and on good and low
  mood days, with their own grids. `/mood` changes today's answers, and
  `/mood-off` turns the whole thing off.
- **Sessions left running while Jiary was closed.** Opening Jiary on one
  asks about the time since you closed it, so `e` ends the session then
  rather than now. A session from an earlier day shows its day beside the
  timer ("since yesterday 17:42").
- The mouse wheel scrolls the week, month and year views.

## Upgrading

Install as before:

```sh
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/barcanjocs/jiary/main/install.sh | sh
```

Your diary gains a `days` table (for sleep and mood) the first time 1.1.0
opens it. Nothing else changes, and nothing needs doing. As ever, copying
`jiary.db` first gives you a backup.

Next to the database there are two new small files: `settings.conf` (your
settings) and `last_seen` (when Jiary last ran). A session already running
when you upgrade isn't asked about the first time, since there's no
`last_seen` yet to measure from.
